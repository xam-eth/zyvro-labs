# Competitor Research — How Game Studio Websites Work

Research date: 3 October 2026. Method: each site's homepage was fetched and summarised for navigation, section order, calls to action, store links, community and press presence. Funnel guidance came from published industry guides. Sources are listed at the end.

**Limits of this research (read before relying on it):**

- Only homepages were read, through an automated summariser. Inner pages, forums, mobile layouts, page speed and real traffic were **not** measured.
- Devolver Digital returned HTTP 403 and was not analysed.
- Traffic, backlink and keyword data per competitor is available through the Semrush tools in this environment and is the recommended next step (see §6).
- Benchmark numbers about Steam wishlists come from third-party guides, not from Valve.

## 2. Patterns that repeat

1. **The games come first.** Every site shows the games or the current game above the fold, not the studio's story.
2. **A direct path to the store.** Store badges or per-platform buttons sit on the game card or in the footer, one click from the first screen (Supercell, Landfall).
3. **A living feed.** News, blog or patch notes drive repeat visits and search traffic (Supercell, Innersloth, Team Cherry, Hello Games). Where the feed is the homepage (Team Cherry), freshness is the whole proposition.
4. **Community as a section, not an afterthought.** Discord and social links appear on every site. Paradox goes furthest with forums, mods and a wiki, and it asks players to create an account.
5. **Press is separated from players.** Landfall puts "Press kits" in the navigation; Supercell's news archive doubles as a press centre; Paradox has a "Media" item. Journalists never have to hunt.
6. **Careers and company pages exist but stay secondary.** Hello Games and Supercell recruit loudly; most indies keep it light.
7. **Personality over polish in indie branding.** Innersloth's mascot and Landfall's playful copy show that a distinct voice matters more than corporate gloss.
8. **Legal and policy pages are present.** Privacy, terms, and for studios with fan communities, fan-creation or modding policies (Innersloth).
9. **Common omissions.** Several well-known indies have no newsletter box (Supercell, Landfall, Team Cherry) and no visible press kit (Team Cherry). That is an opening, not a model to copy: owned channels (email, forum) are the only audience a studio fully controls.

## 3. What the funnel guides add

- **The store page is the marketing.** The decision happens in about three seconds from the capsule image, tags and first screenshot; a recognisable character plus a readable title lifts click-through. A single capsule revision alone has been reported to lift wishlists 30 to 70 percent.
- **Page structure:** top of page sets the fantasy and genre, the middle shows what the player does, the bottom builds confidence through polish and updates.
- **Trailer:** show real gameplay in the first 30 to 60 seconds, no logo cards, no stock music.
- **Screenshots:** 6 to 8, each making one point; no debug overlays, placeholder art, settings screens or menus.
- **Short description:** 160 characters of emotional hook, not specs.
- **Wishlist benchmarks (first week conversion):** about 15 percent under 5K wishlists, 20 percent at 5K to 40K, 23 percent at 40K to 100K, 25 percent above 100K. Page-to-wishlist conversion under 10 percent signals a problem; under 5 percent is critical.
- **Thresholds cited:** around 7,000 wishlists to qualify for Popular Upcoming; 10,000 as a realistic goal; 30,000+ for the top tenth of indie launches.
- **Timing:** put the "Coming Soon" page up as early as possible (guides suggest a year or at least six months before launch). Build a demo three to four months out. Enter Steam Next Fest with at least 2,000 wishlists.
- **Channel ranking (third-party data):** Steam's own surfaces drive about half of post-launch visits; mid-size creators (10K to 100K subscribers) in the game's genre outperform mega-creators and written press; paid ads are generally poor value ($1 to $3 per wishlist) except for retargeting warm audiences near launch.
- **Stack the beats:** trailer, demo update, creator push and festival inside the same few days, not spread over months.

## 4. What press and creators need from a developer website

From the press-kit and developer-website guides:

- **Minimum pages:** Home, a page per game, Press kit, About, Contact. A blog only if it will be updated every few weeks.
- **Press kit contents:** factsheet (developer, publisher, release date, platforms, price, website, social links), short and long descriptions in copy-paste text, screenshots at least 1920×1080 with no watermark, logos as transparent PNG plus vector in light and dark versions, key art, an embedded YouTube trailer, studio details, contact and video-usage permission.
- **Placement:** the press kit link belongs in the **main navigation**, not the footer.
- **Contact:** a plain email address, not only a contact form.
- **Mistakes to avoid:** "Coming Soon" placeholder pages, abandoned blogs, no mobile optimisation, missing HTTPS, autoplaying music or media.
- **Search:** "every Google search for your game's name should lead to a page you control"; a proper domain beats a free subdomain.

## 5. Implications for Zyvro Labs

| Observation | What it means for this site |
|---|---|
| All competitors lead with games and a store path | The Game OS hub already leads with games; it needs real store, play and wishlist buttons on every game card. |
| Living news feed drives return visits | Add a devlog fed by real repository milestones (for example the Last Night siege system, Chain Rider's balance pass). Do not publish a feed that cannot be kept current. |
| Paradox treats forum, mods and wiki as sections | Zyvro needs a real forum to match the brief ("forum etc."). Pair it with Discord rather than replacing it. |
| Press kit in the main navigation | Add a `/press` page and link it from the top bar. |
| Competitors without newsletters leave email unclaimed | A working, consented newsletter is a cheap advantage and the only channel Zyvro owns. |
| Personality beats polish | The dark industrial identity is an asset. Keep it, but never let it hide the store buttons, the trailer or the text. |
| Journalists need assets in under a minute | Ship downloadable zip kits per game, built from the real screenshots and logos. |
| Legal pages are standard | Privacy policy, terms, and an account/data deletion page are required for Google Play anyway. |
